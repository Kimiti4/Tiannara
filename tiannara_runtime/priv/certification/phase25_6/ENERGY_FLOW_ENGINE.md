# Energy Flow Engine

## Purpose
Models the generation, storage, distribution, and consumption of energy across all sources and sectors.

## Energy Sources
- **Fossil** — coal, oil, natural gas (declining under constitution)
- **Nuclear** — fission (current), fusion (future)
- **Solar** — photovoltaic, concentrated solar, solar thermal
- **Wind** — onshore, offshore, airborne
- **Hydro** — river, pumped storage, tidal, wave
- **Geothermal** — deep geothermal, enhanced geothermal
- **Biomass** — biofuels, biogas, waste-to-energy
- **Chemical** — hydrogen, ammonia, synthetic fuels

## Flow Model

### Generation
- Installed capacity (current and planned)
- Capacity factor by source type and location
- Intermittency profiles for variable renewables
- Storage charge/discharge flows

### Distribution
- Electrical grid topology and capacity
- Pipeline networks (gas, hydrogen, heat)
- Fuel transport (tanker, rail, truck)
- Transmission losses

### Consumption
- Industrial energy use
- Residential and commercial
- Transportation
- Agriculture
- Computing and data centers
- Scientific infrastructure
- Energy for energy (extraction/refining losses)

### Storage
- Battery (grid-scale, distributed)
- Pumped hydro
- Thermal storage
- Hydrogen storage
- Mechanical (flywheel, compressed air)

## Analysis
- Energy balance (generation vs. consumption)
- Carbon intensity
- Energy return on investment (EROI)
- Grid stability assessment
- Storage adequacy
- Decarbonization pathway tracking
- Energy security index

## Output
Energy flow accounts, generation mix, consumption breakdown, storage levels, grid stability metrics, decarbonization progress, security alerts.
