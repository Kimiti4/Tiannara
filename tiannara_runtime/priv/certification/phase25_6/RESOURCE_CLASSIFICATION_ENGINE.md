# Resource Classification Engine

## Purpose
Classifies resources along multiple dimensions to inform allocation, sustainability, and risk decisions.

## Classification Dimensions

### Renewability
- **Renewable** — naturally replenished within human timescale (solar, wind, water, biomass)
- **Conditionally renewable** — replenishable if managed sustainably (fisheries, forests, groundwater)
- **Non-renewable** — finite stock (fossil fuels, minerals, rare earths)
- **Recyclable** — can be recovered and reused (metals, some materials)

### Criticality
- **Critical** — essential for civilization function, low substitutability
- **Strategic** — important for long-term development
- **Common** — widely available, easily substituted
- **Luxury** — non-essential for civilization function

### Scarcity
- **Abundant** — supply far exceeds current and projected demand
- **Sufficient** — supply meets demand with reasonable margin
- **Constrained** — supply tight relative to demand
- **Scarce** — supply below demand
- **Depleting** — declining stock with no renewable substitute

### Accessibility
- **Accessible** — available with current technology and infrastructure
- **Conditional** — available but with significant cost or risk
- **Inaccessible** — not available with current technology
- **Future** — potentially available with future technology

### Geopolitical Risk
- **Secure** — geographically diversified or abundant
- **Concentrated** — majority from geopolitically unstable regions
- **Monopolized** — single or few sources control supply

## Classification Updates
Classifications evolve as technology, economy, and ecology change. The engine continuously re-evaluates classifications based on new data.
