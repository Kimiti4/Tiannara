# Institutional Evolution Model

## Purpose

Model the lifecycle and evolution of institutions across civilizational epochs, ensuring institutional identity remains archaeologically recoverable.

## Institutional Lifecycle Events

### Creation
- New institution established to meet emerging needs
- Constitutional authorization documented
- Purpose, responsibilities, capabilities defined
- Initial governance structure established

### Expansion
- Institution scope or capacity increases
- New responsibilities acquired
- Capability portfolio grows
- Governance structure adapts

### Division
- Institution splits into multiple institutions
- Each successor inherits portion of capabilities
- Knowledge and resources distributed
- Lineage preserved for all successors

### Merger
- Multiple institutions combine into one
- Capabilities and knowledge consolidated
- Governance structures unified
- All predecessor lineages preserved

### Retirement
- Institution peacefully ceases operations
- Knowledge archived and transferred
- Responsibilities reassigned
- Complete archaeological record preserved

### Replacement
- Institution succeeded by new institution
- New institution inherits purpose with improved architecture
- Knowledge and capabilities transferred
- Both predecessor and successor preserved

### Preservation
- Institution maintained in original form
- Historical preservation value recognized
- Knowledge continuity function served
- Constitutional heritage preserved

## Lineage Tracking

Each institution maintains:
- Institution ID (persistent across changes)
- Predecessor institution IDs
- Successor institution IDs
- Creation epoch
- Transformation events
- Current state

## Constraints

- Institutional identity must be archaeologically recoverable
- Lineage records become constitutional artifacts
- No institution may be erased — only retired or replaced
