# Civilizational Physics

## Purpose

Define the fundamental laws governing civilizational behavior — the core dynamic equations and interaction models from which all civilizational dynamics emerge.

## Fundamental Laws

### Law of Knowledge Accumulation
Knowledge grows through the interaction of existing knowledge, scientific capital, and discovery opportunities. The rate of accumulation depends on the knowledge base, research capacity, and the frontier of known unknowns.

### Law of Energy-Capability Coupling
Civilizational capability is bounded by accessible energy. Energy capture, conversion efficiency, and distribution infrastructure determine maximum sustainable complexity.

### Law of Institutional Adaptation
Institutions evolve under selection pressure from their environment. Adaptive capacity determines long-term survival. Rigid institutions become brittle and fail during phase transitions.

### Law of Innovation Generation
Innovation rate depends on knowledge diversity, research investment, cross-domain connectivity, and the density of unsolved problems. Innovation is never spontaneous — it emerges from constitutional processes.

### Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns on Complexity
As civilizations grow more complex, the marginal benefit of additional complexity decreases while maintenance costs increase. Optimal complexity balances capability against fragility.

### Law of Resilience-Capacity Tradeoff
Maximum immediate capacity and maximum resilience are fundamentally in tension. Civilizations must allocate resources between production and redundancy.

## Dynamic Interactions

All fundamental laws interact through:
- Feedback loops (reinforcing and balancing)
- Time delays between cause and effect
- Non-linear thresholds and tipping points
- Coupled driver relationships

## Constraints

- All laws must be mathematically expressible
- Parameters must be derived from ontology state
- Laws remain revisable through evidence
- Physics records become constitutional artifacts
