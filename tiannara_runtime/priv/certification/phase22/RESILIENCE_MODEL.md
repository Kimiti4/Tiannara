# Resilience Model

## Purpose

Model civilizational resilience across multiple dimensions — the capacity to absorb disruption, adapt to changing conditions, and maintain core functions.

## Resilience Dimensions

### Scientific Resilience
- Diversity of research programs and methodologies
- Redundancy in critical knowledge domains
- Capacity to regenerate lost scientific capability
- Continuity of scientific institutions through disruption

### Engineering Resilience
- Infrastructure redundancy and failover capability
- Maintenance capacity and spare parts availability
- Engineering knowledge distribution
- Rapid repair and recovery capability

### Institutional Resilience
- Institutional diversity and distributed authority
- Constitutional continuity mechanisms
- Leadership succession and knowledge transfer
- Adaptive governance capacity

### Economic Resilience
- Economic diversity and distributed production
- Resource buffer stocks and strategic reserves
- Supply chain redundancy
- Financial system stability

### Infrastructure Resilience
- Critical infrastructure redundancy
- Distributed vs. centralized architecture balance
- Graceful degradation capability
- Rapid restoration capacity

### Governance Resilience
- Constitutional resilience mechanisms
- Decision-making continuity during crisis
- Multiple independent decision centers
- Recovery governance frameworks

### Ecological Resilience
- Environmental buffer capacity
- Ecosystem service redundancy
- Climate adaptation capacity
- Resource renewal rates

### Educational Resilience
- Distributed educational infrastructure
- Knowledge preservation through teaching
- Multiple knowledge transmission pathways
- Educational continuity mechanisms

## Resilience Measurement

Each dimension measured 0.0–1.0 with composite Civilizational Resilience Index.

## Constraints

- Resilience measurements must be deterministic
- Indicators must be ontology-derived
- Resilience records become constitutional artifacts
