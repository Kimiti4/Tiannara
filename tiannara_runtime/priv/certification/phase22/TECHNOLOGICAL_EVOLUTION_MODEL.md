# Technological Evolution Model

## Purpose

Model the lifecycle of technologies across civilizational epochs — emergence, maturation, integration, replacement, deprecation, and retirement — with all technology linked to scientific evidence.

## Technology Lifecycle

### Emergence
- New technology arising from scientific discovery or engineering innovation
- Initial capability demonstration
- Evidence basis documented
- Readiness level assessment

### Maturation
- Technology capability improves through iteration
- Reliability and efficiency increase
- Manufacturing and deployment scaled
- Performance verified against specifications

### Integration
- Technology adopted into infrastructure and institutions
- Interoperability with existing systems established
- Standards and protocols developed
- Knowledge and skills distributed

### Replacement
- Technology superseded by superior alternative
- Replacement driven by evidence of improvement
- Transition planning for orderly replacement
- Legacy system knowledge preserved

### Deprecation
- Technology no longer actively developed or deployed
- Maintenance continues for existing deployments
- Knowledge preservation initiated
- Replacement pathways documented

### Retirement
- Technology decommissioned from active use
- All systems migrated to alternatives
- Complete knowledge base archived
- Historical significance documented

## Technology- Evidence Linkage

Every technology artifact links to:
- Scientific discoveries that enabled it
- Engineering innovations that developed it
- Performance evidence supporting it
- Failure evidence that led to replacement

## Constraints

- All technology must be linked to scientific evidence
- Technology records become constitutional artifacts
- Retired technology knowledge is preserved
