# Societal Structure Model

## Purpose

Formally describe societal structures within civilizations, remaining descriptive rather than prescriptive, while enabling analysis and simulation.

## Societal Dimensions

### Population
- Total population and distribution
- Demographic characteristics
- Population growth and dynamics
- Settlement patterns (urban, rural, space)

### Communities
- Social organization structures
- Cultural and ethnic groupings
- Professional and interest communities
- Geographic communities

### Education
- Education system structure
- Literacy and skill levels
- Research training capacity
- Lifelong learning infrastructure

### Labor
- Workforce composition
- Employment patterns
- Skill distribution
- Labor mobility

### Health
- Health outcomes and metrics
- Healthcare system capacity
- Disease prevalence and management
- Longevity and quality of life

### Culture
- Cultural diversity and heritage
- Knowledge and arts
- Values and norms
- Cultural transmission mechanisms

### Law and Governance
- Legal frameworks
- Rights and responsibilities
- Justice systems
- Constitutional structures

### Innovation
- Innovation ecosystem structure
- Research and development capacity
- Technology adoption patterns
- Entrepreneurship and enterprise

### Scientific Ecosystem
- Research institution network
- Scientific collaboration patterns
- Knowledge production and dissemination
- Scientific culture and values

## Representation Principles

- Societal structures are described, not prescribed
- Cultural and governance diversity is preserved
- All descriptions are evidence-based
- Societal models are always revisable

## Constraints

- Descriptions must be deterministic
- Societal records become constitutional artifacts
- Prescriptive constraints are not permitted
