# Knowledge and Science Ontology

## Purpose

Formally represent all knowledge domains and scientific systems within civilizations, ensuring knowledge remains permanently linked to its provenance.

## Knowledge Categories

### Scientific Knowledge
- Physics, chemistry, biology, astronomy, geology
- Scientific theories, laws, and models
- Experimental results and observational data
- Scientific methods and protocols

### Engineering Knowledge
- Design principles and methodologies
- Engineering standards and practices
- Material properties and specifications
- System architecture knowledge

### Mathematical Knowledge
- Formal mathematical structures
- Proofs and theorems
- Computational methods
- Mathematical models and simulations

### Educational Knowledge
- Curricula and pedagogical methods
- Educational resources and materials
- Skill development frameworks
- Knowledge transfer protocols

### Operational Knowledge
- Procedures and protocols
- Operational best practices
- Maintenance and repair knowledge
- Emergency response procedures

### Institutional Knowledge
- Organizational structures and processes
- Governance and policy knowledge
- Historical institutional memory
- Cultural and social knowledge

### Historical Knowledge
- Civilization history and records
- Archaeological findings
- Historical analysis and interpretation
- Cultural heritage knowledge

## Knowledge Properties

Each knowledge artifact contains:
- Knowledge ID
- Category and domain
- Content hash
- Provenance chain
- Discovery context
- Validation status
- Confidence level
- Related knowledge links

## Knowledge Preservation

- Knowledge is never destroyed
- Knowledge artifacts are constitutional artifacts
- Knowledge evolution is fully tracked
- Knowledge gaps are explicitly documented

## Constraints

- All knowledge must be linked to provenance
- Knowledge records become constitutional artifacts
- Knowledge gaps are as important as knowledge itself
