# Multi-Layer Twin Architecture

## Purpose

Define the independent but coupled layers of the digital twin, each representing a distinct civilizational subsystem with explicit interaction mechanisms.

## Twin Layers

### Population Layer
- Population size, distribution, demographics
- Growth, migration, urbanization dynamics
- Health, education, skill distributions

### Knowledge Layer
- Accumulated knowledge stock and structure
- Knowledge graph entities and relationships
- Knowledge frontier and gaps

### Science Layer
- Scientific capital and research capacity
- Discovery pipeline and research programs
- Scientific institutions and collaboration networks

### Engineering Layer
- Engineering capital and infrastructure capacity
- Active engineering projects and capabilities
- Engineering knowledge and standards

### Infrastructure Layer
- Infrastructure systems and their states
- Infrastructure dependencies and interdependencies
- Capacity, utilization, and degradation

### Economy Layer
- Economic output, capital stock, investment
- Resource allocation and market mechanisms
- Economic institutions and policies

### Governance Layer
- Governance structures and institutions
- Policy state and constitutional compliance
- Institutional health and effectiveness

### Environment Layer
- Environmental conditions and trends
- Resource base and extraction rates
- Ecological health and ecosystem services

### Technology Layer
- Technology capabilities and maturity levels
- Technology diffusion and adoption
- Technology portfolios and roadmaps

### Institution Layer
- Institution entities, relationships, and health
- Institutional adaptation and evolution
- Inter-institutional coordination

## Layer Interactions

Each pair of layers has defined interaction mechanisms:
- Knowledge drives Science and Engineering
- Infrastructure enables Economy and Population
- Governance regulates all layers
- Environment constrains Population and Economy
- Technology amplifies Engineering and Science

## Constraints

- Layer interactions must be explicit and deterministic
- Cross-layer consistency must be verifiable
- Each layer remains independently replayable
- Layer records become constitutional artifacts
