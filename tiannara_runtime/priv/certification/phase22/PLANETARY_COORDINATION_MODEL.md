# Planetary Coordination Model

## Purpose

Represent and coordinate global-scale civilization activities including research, infrastructure, logistics, energy, scientific collaboration, engineering collaboration, disaster response, and knowledge distribution.

## Coordination Domains

### Global Research Coordination
- Distributed research agenda alignment
- Shared laboratory and facility access
- Cross-institutional scientific programs
- Global peer review and validation

### Global Infrastructure Coordination
- Transcontinental infrastructure planning
- Shared resource allocation
- Interdependent infrastructure management
- Global standards and interoperability

### Planetary Logistics Coordination
- Global supply chain management
- Resource distribution networks
- Emergency response logistics
- Long-haul transportation coordination

### Global Energy Coordination
- Continental grid integration
- Energy trading and balancing
- Cross-border renewable deployment
- Backup and redundancy sharing

### Scientific Collaboration
- Global scientific data sharing
- Distributed simulation networks
- Joint research programs
- Open science infrastructure

### Engineering Collaboration
- Large-scale engineering coordination
- Shared manufacturing capacity
- Collaborative technology development
- Engineering standards harmonization

### Disaster Response
- Global early warning systems
- Coordinated emergency response
- Multi-national recovery operations
- Resilience resource sharing

### Knowledge Distribution
- Universal education access
- Open knowledge repositories
- Translation and accessibility
- Knowledge preservation systems

## Coordination Architecture

Coordination remains decentralized where appropriate, with constitutional oversight where necessary.

- **Local Autonomy**: Civilizations retain internal governance authority
- **Global Cooperation**: Coordination on shared challenges
- **Constitutional Constraints**: All coordination respects constitutional principles
- **Deterministic Lineage**: All coordination interactions are replayable

## Coordination Structure

Each coordination artifact contains:

- **Coordination ID**: Content-addressed identifier
- **Domain**: Coordination domain
- **Participating Civilizations**: Involved parties
- **Coordination Mechanism**: How coordination is achieved
- **Agreements**: Formal coordination agreements
- **Shared Resources**: Resources allocated to coordination
- **Coordination Metrics**: Effectiveness measurements
- **Fingerprint**: Deterministic content hash

## Constraints

- Coordination must preserve civilizational autonomy
- Coordination records become constitutional artifacts
- Coordination mechanisms are transparent and replayable
