# Knowledge Accumulation Model

## Purpose

Model the constitutional accumulation of knowledge through discovery, validation, integration, preservation, reuse, and transmission.

## Knowledge Lifecycle

### Discovery
- New knowledge generated through research, experimentation, observation
- Discovery rate depends on scientific capital, existing knowledge, frontier density
- Every discovery linked to provenance

### Validation
- Knowledge validated through replication, peer review, practical application
- Validation confidence tracked explicitly
- Unvalidated knowledge never enters canonical knowledge base

### Integration
- New knowledge integrated with existing knowledge framework
- Integration may modify existing understanding
- Contradictions preserved until resolved by further evidence

### Preservation
- Knowledge preserved in constitutional archives
- Multiple redundant storage ensures survival
- Knowledge never deleted — only superseded

### Reuse
- Knowledge applied to new problems and domains
- Reuse patterns tracked for impact measurement
- Knowledge accessibility determines reuse rate

### Transmission
- Knowledge transferred across generations
- Educational systems as primary transmission mechanism
- Knowledge loss through transmission failure tracked

## Knowledge Loss Mechanisms

- Institutional memory decay (personnel, documentation, culture)
- Infrastructure loss (archives, libraries, databases)
- Language and skill obsolescence
- Educational curriculum degradation
- Generational knowledge transfer failure

## Measurement

- Knowledge stock (total validated knowledge)
- Knowledge growth rate
- Knowledge diversity index
- Knowledge fragmentation index
- Knowledge loss rate
- Knowledge reuse frequency
- Knowledge transmission fidelity

## Constraints

- Knowledge accumulation must be deterministic
- All knowledge must be provenance-linked
- Knowledge records become constitutional artifacts
