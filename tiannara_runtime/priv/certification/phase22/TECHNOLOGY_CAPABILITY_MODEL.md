# Technology Capability Model

## Purpose

Formally represent technological capabilities across all domains, with explicit maturity levels and development trajectories.

## Technology Domains

### Manufacturing
- Traditional manufacturing
- Additive manufacturing (3D printing)
- Precision manufacturing
- Nanofabrication
- Self-replicating systems

### Robotics and Automation
- Industrial robotics
- Autonomous systems
- Swarm robotics
- Humanoid robotics
- Space robotics

### Artificial Intelligence
- Machine learning systems
- Cognitive architectures
- Autonomous reasoning
- Knowledge representation
- Multi-agent systems

### Biotechnology
- Genetic engineering
- Synthetic biology
- Medical biotechnology
- Bio-manufacturing
- Environmental biotechnology

### Energy Technology
- Advanced generation
- Energy storage
- Transmission and distribution
- Fusion technology
- Space-based power

### Transportation
- Ground and marine
- Aviation
- Space launch
- Orbital transit
- Interplanetary propulsion

### Materials Science
- Structural materials
- Functional materials
- Smart materials
- Metamaterials
- Self-healing materials

### Computing
- Classical computing
- Quantum computing
- Neuromorphic computing
- Optical computing
- Distributed computing

### Aerospace
- Orbital systems
- Planetary systems
- Deep space systems
- Propulsion systems
- Life support systems

## Technology Maturity Levels

- **Level 0 — Theoretical**: Mathematical possibility established
- **Level 1 — Conceptual**: Design concepts exist
- **Level 2 — Experimental**: Laboratory demonstration
- **Level 3 — Prototype**: Working prototype exists
- **Level 4 — Operational**: Deployed and operational
- **Level 5 — Optimized**: Performance optimized
- **Level 6 — Transformative**: Enables new capabilities

## Constraints

- Technology maturity must be explicitly documented
- Technology records become constitutional artifacts
- Technology dependencies must be fully mapped
