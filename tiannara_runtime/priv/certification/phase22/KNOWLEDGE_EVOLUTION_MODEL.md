# Knowledge Evolution Model

## Purpose

Model how knowledge evolves across epochs — discovery, refinement, generalization, specialization, integration, and preservation — ensuring knowledge never loses provenance.

## Knowledge Lifecycle

### Discovery
- New knowledge generated through research and experimentation
- Every discovery linked to provenance chain
- Discovery context preserved
- Discovery confidence tracked

### Refinement
- Existing knowledge improved through validation
- Precision and accuracy increased
- Error bounds reduced
- Refinement preserves original discovery lineage

### Generalization
- Knowledge extended to broader contexts
- Unified theories encompass multiple phenomena
- Generalization preserves specific instances
- Limits of generalization documented

### Specialization
- Knowledge deepened in specific domains
- Specialization builds on general foundations
- Specialized knowledge linked to general principles
- Inter-specialization connections maintained

### Integration
- Knowledge from multiple domains synthesized
- Cross-domain connections established
- Integration produces new understanding
- Component knowledge preserved

### Preservation
- Knowledge maintained across generations
- Redundant storage ensures survival
- Format migration prevents obsolescence
- Knowledge never deleted — only superseded

## Knowledge Provenance

Every knowledge artifact contains:
- Provenance chain (complete discovery-to-current path)
- Validation history
- Integration records
- Confidence trajectory
- Related knowledge links
- Supersession records (if superseded)

## Constraints

- Knowledge must never lose provenance
- Knowledge records become constitutional artifacts
- Superseded knowledge is preserved, not deleted
