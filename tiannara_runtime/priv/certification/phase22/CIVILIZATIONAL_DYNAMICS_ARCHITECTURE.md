# Constitutional Civilizational Dynamics Architecture

## Overview

The Constitutional Civilizational Dynamics System (CCDS) defines the fundamental laws governing the behavior, growth, stability, adaptation, innovation, and decline of civilizations. Where Phase 22.0 defines what a civilization is, Phase 22.1 defines how civilizations behave over time. This architecture becomes the dynamic engine that later powers digital twins, simulation, planetary intelligence, Kardashev transition planning, and constitutional governance optimization.

## Constitutional Principle

Civilizations are dynamic adaptive systems. They evolve through lawful interactions among people, institutions, knowledge, energy, resources, infrastructure, governance, and technology. Civilizational dynamics shall be deterministic, mathematically grounded, evidence-driven, constitutionally governed, replayable, and archaeologically reconstructible.

## Dynamic Hierarchy

```
Civilization
  └── State
       └── Drivers
            └── Interactions
                 └── Transitions
                      └── Events
                           └── Consequences
                                └── New State
```

Every transition preserves replay lineage.

## Architecture Components

### Civilizational Physics
- Fundamental laws governing civilizational behavior
- Core dynamic equations and interaction models

### Growth Engine
- Deterministic growth through knowledge, energy, engineering, institutions, education, and technology
- No hard-coded exponential assumptions

### Innovation Dynamics
- Innovation as a constitutional process with provenance
- Sources: research, engineering, cross-domain synthesis, mathematics, experimentation, simulation

### Knowledge Accumulation Model
- Discovery, validation, integration, preservation, reuse, transmission
- Constitutional knowledge accumulation

### Resilience Model
- Scientific, engineering, institutional, economic, infrastructure, governance, ecological, educational resilience
- Measurable resilience dimensions

### Collapse and Recovery Model
- Constitutional failure modes and recovery mechanisms
- Archaeology preserved through collapse

### State Transitions
- Expansion, stabilization, transformation, contraction, recovery, reorganization
- Deterministic transition conditions

## Integration Points

- **Input**: Phase 22.0 ontology (civilization representations)
- **Output**: Dynamic models for Phase 22.2 (digital twin) and subsequent phases
- **Foundation**: CSOS v1.0 constitutional guarantees

## Acceptance Criteria

- Civilizational dynamics are constitutionally specified
- Growth mechanisms remain deterministic
- Innovation preserves provenance
- Collapse preserves archaeology
- Recovery remains evidence-based
- Replay reconstructs dynamic evolution
- Archaeology reconstructs all transitions
- No runtime implementation exists
