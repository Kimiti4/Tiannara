# Domain Relationship Model

## Purpose

Formally represent scientific and engineering domains, their boundaries, internal structures, and the relationships connecting them — enabling systematic cross-domain discovery and synthesis.

## Domain Representation

Each domain is represented by:

- **Domain ID**: Content-addressed identifier
- **Domain Name**: Canonical domain name
- **Scope**: Phenomena, systems, and questions within the domain
- **Core Concepts**: Fundamental concepts in the domain
- **Fundamental Laws**: Governing laws and principles
- **Methodologies**: Standard research methodologies
- **Boundary Conditions**: Where the domain interfaces with others
- **Active Questions**: Open scientific questions
- **Confidence**: Overall confidence in domain knowledge

## Relationship Types

### Shared Foundations
Domains share underlying mathematical or physical principles.

### Causal Connection
Causal relationships flow between domains (e.g., Physics → Chemistry).

### Methodological Transfer
Methodologies from one domain apply in another.

### Structural Isomorphism
Similar structures appear in different domains.

### Emergent Relationship
Higher-domain phenomena emerge from lower-domain configurations.

### Analogical Relationship
Domains exhibit analogous behavior despite different substrates.

### Instrumental Relationship
One domain provides tools or instruments for another.

## Relationship Properties

- **Relationship ID**: Content-addressed identifier
- **Source Domains**: Originating domains
- **Target Domains**: Receiving domains
- **Evidence**: Evidence supporting the relationship
- **Shared Principles**: Principles common to both domains
- **Shared Mathematics**: Mathematical structures in common
- **Shared Engineering Concepts**: Engineering concepts in common
- **Confidence**: Confidence in relationship validity (0.0–1.0)
- **Replay Root**: Hash for deterministic reconstruction
- **Archaeology Root**: Hash for lineage reconstruction

## Relationship Discovery

1. **Domain Mapping**: Formal representation of each domain
2. **Cross-Domain Comparison**: Systematic comparison of domain structures
3. **Pattern Detection**: Identify recurring patterns across domains
4. **Hypothesis Formation**: Formulate cross-domain relationship hypothesis
5. **Evidence Collection**: Gather evidence from both domains
6. **Validation**: Test hypothesis against evidence
7. **Relationship Recording**: Record with full lineage

## Constraints

- Domain relationships require evidence from all involved domains
- Relationship records become constitutional artifacts
- Relationship confidence reflects evidence strength
