# Theory Archaeology Model

## Purpose

Define the archaeology model for the theory ecosystem — enabling reconstruction of why each theory emerged, which evidence supported it, which theories competed, why one dominated, why it was replaced, and what knowledge survived.

## Archaeological Questions

### Emergence
- Why did this theory emerge?
- What evidence or anomaly motivated it?
- What prior theories did it build on?
- What intellectual context existed?

### Support
- Which evidence supported the theory?
- What predictions confirmed it?
- What engineering successes validated it?
- What mathematical consistency was established?

### Competition
- Which theories competed with it?
- What were the key competition outcomes?
- Why did it succeed or fail in competition?
- What minority positions were preserved?

### Dominance
- Why did it become dominant?
- What evidence solidified its position?
- What predictions distinguished it?
- What engineering utility confirmed it?

### Replacement
- Why was it replaced?
- What new evidence contradicted it?
- What superior explanation emerged?
- What engineering failures accumulated?

### Survival
- What knowledge survived from the theory?
- What concepts were carried forward?
- What mathematical tools persisted?
- What engineering practices continued?

## Archaeology Structure

Each archaeology record contains:

- **Theory ID**: Theory being archaeologically analyzed
- **Emergence Context**: Why and how the theory emerged
- **Evidence Record**: Evidence supporting the theory
- **Competition History**: Complete competition history
- **Dominance Period**: When and why it dominated
- **Replacement History**: How and why it was replaced
- **Knowledge Legacy**: What survived and persisted
- **Complete Lineage**: Full evolutionary lineage
- **Fingerprint**: Deterministic content hash

## Archaeology Completeness

Archaeology is complete when all archaeological questions are answered with deterministic references to original constitutional artifacts.
