# Priority Governance Framework

## Purpose

Define the constitutional governance structure for scientific prioritization decisions — who decides, what authority they hold, how decisions are reviewed, and how human governance is preserved.

## Governance Levels

### Constitutional Level
- Priority weights are constitutionally defined
- Constitutional amendments require civilizational consensus
- Priority principles are constitutional artifacts

### Scientific Governance Level
- Scientific priority decisions are governed by scientific program governance (Phase 22.X.1)
- Priority evaluation follows constitutionally defined factors
- Scientific boards review and approve priority decisions

### Engineering Governance Level
- Engineering priority decisions follow constitutional priority factors
- Engineering review boards approve engineering resource allocation
- Cross-domain coordination governed by constitutional framework

### Human Governance Level
- Human authority overrides all automated priority decisions
- Human override documentation is constitutionally required
- Human governance preserves civilizational democratic processes

## Governance Principles

### Transparency
All priority decisions, including rationale, evidence, and weights, are publicly accessible constitutional artifacts.

### Accountability
Every priority decision is attributed to a governance authority with clear responsibility.

### Reviewability
Priority decisions may be reviewed, appealed, and revised through constitutional processes.

### Fairness
Priority distributions across domains, methodologies, and institutions are monitored for fairness.

### Sustainability
Priority decisions consider long-term sustainability across generations.

## Governance Processes

### Priority Weight Amendment
1. Proposed change to constitutional weights
2. Impact assessment of weight change
3. Review by constitutional governance
4. Public comment and review period
5. Approval or rejection with rationale
6. Amendment recorded as constitutional artifact

### Priority Decision Appeal
1. Appeal filed with rationale and evidence
2. Governance review of original decision
3. Re-evaluation of priority factors
4. Decision upheld, modified, or overturned
5. Appeal record preserved with lineage

### Priority Audit
1. Periodic audit of priority decisions
2. Random sample review
3. Consistency and fairness assessment
4. Audit report as constitutional artifact
5. Recommendations for improvement

## Constraints

- Governance records are constitutional artifacts
- Human authority is always preserved
- All governance actions are replayable
