# Knowledge Generalization Model

## Purpose

Discover broader applicability for existing knowledge, validate generalizations across contexts, and assess generalization confidence.

## Generalization Types

### Domain Generalization
- Knowledge validated in domain A may apply to domain B
- Cross-domain transfer of principles
- Abstraction above domain specifics

### Context Generalization
- Knowledge validated in context X may apply to context Y
- Removal of context-specific constraints
- Identification of context-invariant aspects

### Scale Generalization
- Knowledge validated at scale S may apply at scale S'
- Cross-scale invariance discovery
- Scale transformation rules

### Temporal Generalization
- Knowledge validated at time T may apply at time T'
- Temporal invariance assessment
- Evolution-robust knowledge identification

## Generalization Process

1. **Candidate Identification**: Identify knowledge for generalization
2. **Target Scope Definition**: Define potential broader scope
3. **Prediction Derivation**: Derive predictions in new scope
4. **Empirical Testing**: Test predictions with evidence
5. **Confidence Update**: Adjust confidence based on results
6. **Scope Formalization**: Formalize generalized scope
7. **Generalization Recording**: Record with full lineage

## Confidence in Generalization

- **Direct Evidence**: Evidence in the new scope
- **Analogical Strength**: Similarity between original and new scope
- **Causal Mechanism**: Whether causal mechanism generalizes
- **Boundary Proximity**: How close to known boundaries

## Constraints

- Generalizations must be empirically testable
- Generalization records become constitutional artifacts
- Generalization confidence reflects available evidence
