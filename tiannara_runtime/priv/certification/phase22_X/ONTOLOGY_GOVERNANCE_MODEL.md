# Ontology Governance Model

## Purpose

Define the constitutional governance structure for ontology evolution — ensuring that all concept changes, hierarchy reorganizations, merge and split operations, and version transitions follow constitutional principles.

## Governance Levels

### Constitutional Level
- Ontology hierarchy levels are constitutionally defined
- Semantic validation criteria are constitutional
- Backward compatibility is constitutionally mandated
- Ontology evolution principles are constitutional artifacts

### Scientific Governance Level
- Concept evolution governed by domain experts
- Merge and split decisions governed by evidence assessment
- Hierarchy reorganization requires scientific review
- Semantic validation conducted by constitutional bodies

### Human Governance Level
- Human authority overrides all ontology changes
- Human oversight of major ontology restructures
- Democratic processes for hierarchy level changes
- Human governance of concept deprecation

## Governance Principles

### Evidence Sovereignty
- Ontology changes must be evidence-driven
- No concept may be merged without evidence convergence
- No concept may be split without evidence divergence
- Hierarchy changes require evidence justification

### Transparency
- All ontology changes are publicly documented
- Change rationale is preserved
- Governance decisions are constitutional artifacts

### Backward Compatibility
- Ontology changes must not invalidate prior knowledge
- Legacy concept references must remain resolvable
- Deprecated concepts must map to current equivalents

## Governance Processes

### Ontology Change Approval
1. Change proposal with evidence
2. Semantic validation
3. Impact assessment
4. Constitutional compliance check
5. Approval or rejection with rationale
6. Change recording

### Ontology Version Certification
1. Version snapshot preparation
2. Full semantic validation
3. Backward compatibility verification
4. Constitutional review
5. Certification or rejection
6. Version recording

## Constraints

- Governance records are constitutional artifacts
- Human authority is always preserved
- All governance actions are replayable
