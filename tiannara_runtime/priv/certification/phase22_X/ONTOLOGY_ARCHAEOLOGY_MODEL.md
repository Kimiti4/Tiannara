# Ontology Archaeology Model

## Purpose

Define the archaeology model for ontology evolution — enabling reconstruction of why each concept was introduced, why it was renamed, why it was merged or split, which discoveries caused ontology evolution, and how semantic understanding evolved.

## Archaeological Questions

### Concept Introduction
- Why was this concept introduced?
- What evidence or discovery motivated it?
- What gap in ontology did it fill?
- What prior concepts did it relate to?

### Concept Renaming
- Why was the concept renamed?
- What semantic shift occurred?
- What evidence caused the rename?
- What was the previous name and lineage?

### Concept Merging
- Why were concepts merged?
- What convergence triggered the merge?
- What definitions converged?
- What evidence supported unification?

### Concept Splitting
- Why was the concept split?
- What divergence triggered the split?
- What meanings separated?
- What evidence supported separation?

### Ontology Evolution Causes
- Which discoveries caused ontology evolution?
- What new domains emerged?
- What theoretical advances drove changes?
- What engineering needs reshaped the ontology?

### Semantic Understanding Evolution
- How has semantic understanding evolved?
- What definitions changed over time?
- What relationships were discovered or modified?
- What hierarchy reorganizations occurred?

## Archaeology Structure

Each archaeology record contains:

- **Ontology Period**: Time period analyzed
- **Concept Histories**: Evolution of individual concepts
- **Merge Records**: Historical merge operations
- **Split Records**: Historical split operations
- **Evolution Drivers**: Discoveries driving changes
- **Semantic Trajectory**: How understanding evolved
- **Key Decisions**: Major ontology decisions
- **Fingerprint**: Deterministic content hash

## Archaeology Completeness

Archaeology is complete when all archaeological questions are answered with deterministic references to original constitutional artifacts.
