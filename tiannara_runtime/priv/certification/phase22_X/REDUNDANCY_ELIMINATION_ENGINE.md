# Redundancy Elimination Engine

## Purpose

Detect and eliminate redundancy in the knowledge base through duplicate detection, equivalent concept merging, hierarchical simplification, and obsolete knowledge retirement — with strict lineage preservation.

## Redundancy Types

### Exact Duplicates
- Identical knowledge artifacts
- Same content, different identifiers
- Automatic detection and merging

### Near Duplicates
- Slightly different formulations of same knowledge
- Different contexts, same substance
- Similarity-based detection

### Equivalent Concepts
- Different terms for same concept
- Cross-domain terminological variation
- Semantic equivalence detection

### Hierarchical Redundancy
- Knowledge subsumed by more general principle
- Specific instance of general law
- Hierarchical simplification

### Obsolete Knowledge
- Superseded by better knowledge
- Refuted by new evidence
- No longer scientifically valid

## Redundancy Detection Methods

### Content-Based Duplicate Detection
- Semantic similarity computation
- Structural isomorphism detection
- Mathematical equivalence verification

### Usage-Based Redundancy Detection
- Low-usage knowledge artifact identification
- Redundant coverage analysis
- Cross-reference analysis

### Hierarchy-Based Simplification
- Generalization-specialization relationship analysis
- Abstraction level optimization
- Knowledge compaction

## Elimination Process

1. **Detection**: Identify redundant knowledge
2. **Verification**: Confirm redundancy is genuine
3. **Impact Analysis**: Assess impact of elimination
4. **Merge/Retire**: Apply appropriate elimination
5. **Reference Update**: Update all references
6. **Lineage Preservation**: Record elimination in lineage
7. **Verification**: Confirm no information lost

## Constraints

- No evidence may be discarded without lineage
- Elimination records become constitutional artifacts
- Eliminated knowledge remains archaeologically recoverable
