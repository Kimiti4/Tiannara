# Ontology Hierarchy Model

## Purpose

Define the constitutional hierarchy of the universal ontology — from reality through scientific domains, subdomains, concept families, concepts, properties, relationships, evidence, and observations.

## Hierarchy Levels

### Level 1: Reality
- The totality of existence
- Root of all ontology
- Immutable constitutional reference

### Level 2: Scientific Domains
- Major domains of scientific inquiry
- Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Geology, Astronomy, Mathematics, Computer Science, Engineering, Social Sciences, Humanities
- Each domain has constitutional recognition

### Level 3: Subdomains
- Subdivisions within scientific domains
- Example: Physics → Quantum Mechanics, Thermodynamics, Relativity, Optics, Acoustics
- Domain-specific hierarchy

### Level 4: Concept Families
- Groups of related concepts
- Example: Quantum Mechanics → Wave Functions, Observables, Operators, States
- Family-level organization

### Level 5: Concepts
- Individual scientific concepts
- Each concept has a unique content-addressed identifier
- Definition, evidence, confidence, dependencies

### Level 6: Properties
- Attributes and properties of concepts
- Quantitative and qualitative properties
- Property constraints and ranges

### Level 7: Relationships
- Relationships between concepts
- Types: causal, hierarchical, temporal, spatial, mathematical, empirical
- Relationship strength and direction

### Level 8: Evidence
- Evidence supporting ontology nodes
- Observation references, experimental results, predictions
- Evidence provenance and quality

### Level 9: Observations
- Raw observational data
- Measurement results, experimental outcomes
- Ground truth references

## Hierarchy Rules

- Every node has a unique content-addressed identifier
- Every node preserves parent lineage
- Hierarchy levels are strictly enforced
- Cross-level relationships are explicitly defined
- Hierarchy changes preserve backward compatibility
