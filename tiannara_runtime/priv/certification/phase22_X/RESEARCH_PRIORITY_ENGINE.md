# Research Priority Engine

## Purpose

Evaluate research candidates using multi-factor priority analysis: scientific importance, engineering leverage, discovery multiplier, cross-domain influence, resource efficiency, time horizon, risk, constitutional alignment, knowledge gap reduction, and expected civilizational benefit.

## Priority Factors

### Scientific Importance
- Fundamental knowledge advancement potential
- Paradigm shift likelihood
- Scientific question significance
- Knowledge gap magnitude

### Engineering Leverage
- Engineering capability enablement
- Technology development acceleration
- Infrastructure improvement potential
- Practical application breadth

### Discovery Multiplier
- How many downstream discoveries become possible
- How many engineering capabilities unlock
- How many domains benefit
- How much uncertainty decreases

### Cross-Domain Influence
- Cross-domain applicability breadth
- Interdisciplinary synthesis potential
- Methodological transfer value
- Knowledge unification potential

### Resource Efficiency
- Expected discovery per unit resource
- Time to first meaningful result
- Resource scalability
- Alternative use opportunity cost

### Time Horizon
- Short-term (1–5 years) discovery potential
- Medium-term (5–25 years) impact
- Long-term (25–100+ years) value
- Generational knowledge value

### Risk
- Technical feasibility risk
- Scientific hypothesis risk
- Resource overrun risk
- Opportunity cost risk

### Constitutional Alignment
- Constitutional compliance
- Ethical governance compatibility
- Sustainability alignment
- Human benefit assurance

### Knowledge Gap Reduction
- Size of knowledge gap being addressed
- Criticality of gap to further progress
- Gap persistence if unaddressed
- Gap uniqueness (other programs addressing it)

### Civilizational Benefit
- Expected improvement in civilizational outcomes
- Resilience enhancement
- Sustainability contribution
- Quality of life impact

## Priority Calculation

```
Priority = Σ(wi × fi)
```

Where wi are constitutionally governed weights and fi are normalized factor scores.

## Priority Re-evaluation

Priorities re-evaluated when:
- New significant evidence arrives
- Discovery multiplier estimates change
- Resource availability changes
- Constitutional priorities updated
- Portfolio rebalancing triggered

## Constraints

- Priority decisions are evidence-driven
- Priority records become constitutional artifacts
- Priority weights are constitutionally governed
