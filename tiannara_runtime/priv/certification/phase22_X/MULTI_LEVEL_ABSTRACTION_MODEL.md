# Multi-Level Abstraction Model

## Purpose

Define the formal abstraction hierarchy from raw observations through unified understanding, with level-specific compression rules, fidelity requirements, and cross-level transformation protocols.

## Abstraction Levels

### Level 0: Observations
- Raw sensor data, experimental measurements, field observations
- No abstraction applied
- Maximum information, minimum structure

### Level 1: Evidence
- Curated, validated observations
- Uncertainty quantified
- Provenance attached
- Initial structure imposed

### Level 2: Information
- Structured evidence with context
- Relationships between evidence identified
- Metadata and semantics attached
- First level of organized abstraction

### Level 3: Knowledge
- Contextualized information with meaning
- Causal relationships identified
- Domain-specific understanding
- Actionable insights possible

### Level 4: Patterns
- Recurring structures detected
- Statistical regularities formalized
- Temporal and spatial patterns
- Cross-evidence pattern matching

### Level 5: Models
- Predictive abstractions from patterns
- Formal mathematical/computational representations
- Testable predictions
- Model uncertainty quantified

### Level 6: Principles
- Reusable general rules extracted from models
- Cross-context validity
- Evidence-backed generalizations
- Principle strength quantified

### Level 7: Scientific Laws
- Mathematically formulated universal principles
- Broad domain applicability
- Strong evidence foundation
- Boundary conditions defined

### Level 8: Engineering Insights
- Practically applicable principles
- Design rules and heuristics
- Engineering trade-off knowledge
- Implementation guidance

### Level 9: Unified Understanding
- Cross-domain integration of laws and principles
- Fundamental causal relationships
- Unified explanatory frameworks
- Highest abstraction level

## Cross-Level Transformation Rules

Each level transition defines:
- **Input**: Artifacts from lower level
- **Output**: Artifacts at higher level
- **Transformation**: How abstraction is achieved
- **Fidelity Requirement**: Minimum information preservation
- **Verification**: How transformation correctness is verified

## Constraints

- Transformations preserve provenance across all levels
- Fidelity thresholds are constitutionally governed
- Higher levels remain traceable to lower levels
