# Law Discovery Model

## Purpose

Induce general scientific laws from validated principles, formulate them mathematically, and identify their boundary conditions and domains of applicability.

## Law Structure

Each scientific law contains:

- **Law ID**: Content-addressed identifier
- **Statement**: Concise formulation of the law
- **Supporting Principles**: Principles that support the law
- **Supporting Evidence**: Direct evidence for the law
- **Mathematical Representation**: Formal mathematical expression
- **Applicable Domains**: Domains governed by the law
- **Boundary Conditions**: Limits of applicability
- **Confidence**: Confidence in law validity (0.0–1.0)
- **Prediction Accuracy**: Measured predictive performance
- **Replay Root**: Hash for deterministic reconstruction
- **Archaeology Root**: Hash for lineage reconstruction

## Law Discovery Process

1. **Principle Clustering**: Group related principles
2. **Abstraction Search**: Search for unifying abstraction
3. **Mathematical Formulation**: Express as mathematical relationship
4. **Prediction Derivation**: Derive testable predictions
5. **Empirical Testing**: Test against evidence
6. **Boundary Determination**: Identify applicability limits
7. **Peer Review**: Cross-validation against known science
8. **Law Certification**: Constitutionally certified law

## Law Types

### Fundamental Laws
- Universal within domain
- Mathematically precise
- Exceptionless within boundary conditions

### Phenomenological Laws
- Empirically observed regularities
- May lack complete mechanistic explanation
- Reliable within observed range

### Statistical Laws
- Probabilistic regularities
- Apply to populations, not individuals
- Quantified uncertainty

### Emergent Laws
- Apply at higher abstraction levels
- Not reducible to lower-level laws
- Arise from collective behavior

## Constraints

- Laws must be empirically testable
- Law records become constitutional artifacts
- Laws are subject to revision with new evidence
