# Multi-Domain Validation Model

## Purpose

Validate synthesized cross-domain relationships across all involved domains, ensuring scientific correctness, engineering utility, mathematical consistency, evidence sufficiency, cross-domain reproducibility, and constitutional compliance.

## Validation Dimensions

### Scientific Correctness
- Does the relationship hold in each domain?
- Are there counterexamples in any domain?
- Is the relationship consistent with domain-specific laws?
- Does the relationship make testable predictions?

### Engineering Utility
- Does the relationship enable engineering applications?
- Can it be used for design or optimization?
- Does it offer practical improvements?
- Is it transferable to real-world systems?

### Mathematical Consistency
- Is the relationship mathematically well-formed?
- Are mathematical transformations between domains valid?
- Are numerical predictions consistent?
- Does the mathematics generalize across domains?

### Evidence Sufficiency
- Is there sufficient evidence in each domain?
- Is evidence quality adequate?
- Are there conflicting evidence sources?
- Is the evidence reproducible?

### Cross-Domain Reproducibility
- Can the relationship be demonstrated in each domain?
- Are experimental protocols transferable?
- Are results consistent across domain-specific measurements?
- Is the relationship robust to domain-specific variations?

### Constitutional Compliance
- Does the synthesis respect constitutional principles?
- Is human authority preserved?
- Is the synthesis transparent and auditable?
- Are limitations documented?

## Validation Process

1. **Validation Planning**: Design validation strategy per domain
2. **Evidence Collection**: Gather evidence from all domains
3. **Analysis**: Analyze evidence against validation dimensions
4. **Scoring**: Score each validation dimension (0.0–1.0)
5. **Overall Assessment**: Aggregate across dimensions
6. **Certification Decision**: Pass, pass with conditions, fail
7. **Recording**: Record validation with full lineage

## Constraints

- Validation requires evidence from all involved domains
- Validation records become constitutional artifacts
- Failed validation blocks cross-domain knowledge integration
