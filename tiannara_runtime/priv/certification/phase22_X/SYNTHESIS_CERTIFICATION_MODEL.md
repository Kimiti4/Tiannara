# Synthesis Certification Model

## Purpose

Certify cross-domain syntheses as constitutionally valid, ensuring every synthesized relationship meets requirements for scientific correctness, engineering utility, mathematical consistency, evidence sufficiency, cross-domain reproducibility, and constitutional compliance.

## Certification Criteria

### Scientific Correctness
- Relationship holds in all involved domains
- No counterexamples in any domain
- Consistent with domain-specific knowledge
- Testable predictions confirmed

### Engineering Utility
- Enables practical applications
- Transferable to real systems
- Measurable improvement over single-domain approaches

### Mathematical Consistency
- Well-formed mathematical structure
- Valid cross-domain transformations
- Numerically consistent predictions

### Evidence Sufficiency
- Adequate evidence in every domain
- Evidence quality meets standards
- Reproducible results

### Cross-Domain Reproducibility
- Demonstrable in all domains
- Protocols transferable
- Robust to domain variations

### Constitutional Compliance
- Constitutional principles preserved
- Human authority maintained
- Transparent and auditable
- Limitations documented

## Certification Process

1. **Submission**: Synthesis submitted for certification
2. **Evidence Review**: Evidence package reviewed against criteria
3. **Validation Assessment**: Validation results evaluated
4. **Certification Decision**: Pass, pass with conditions, fail
5. **Certification Documentation**: Complete certification record
6. **Knowledge Integration**: If passed, integrate into knowledge base

## Certification Outcomes

- **Certified**: Full certification, knowledge integrated
- **Certified with Conditions**: Certified with required follow-up actions
- **Failed**: Certification denied, synthesis may be resubmitted after improvement

## Certification Structure

Each certification contains:

- **Certification ID**: Content-addressed identifier
- **Synthesis ID**: Synthesis being certified
- **Criteria Scores**: Score per certification criterion
- **Overall Decision**: Certified, conditional, failed
- **Conditions**: Required actions if conditional
- **Evidence Package**: Evidence supporting certification
- **Certifying Authority**: Constitutional governance reference
- **Fingerprint**: Deterministic content hash

## Constraints

- Certification is deterministic and evidence-based
- Certification records become constitutional artifacts
- Certifications are themselves replayable
