# Natural Hazard Engine

## Purpose

Define the engine that assesses risks from natural hazards.

## Hazard Types

- **Seismic** — Earthquakes, tsunamis, volcanic eruptions.
- **Meteorological** — Hurricanes, cyclones, typhoons, tornadoes, hailstorms.
- **Hydrological** — Floods, flash floods, storm surges.
- **Climatological** — Droughts, heatwaves, cold waves, wildfires.
- **Geological** — Landslides, avalanches, sinkholes, coastal erosion.
- **Biological** — Pandemics, epidemics, infestations.
- **Extraterrestrial** — Solar flares, geomagnetic storms, asteroid impacts.

## Assessment Methodology

For each hazard, the engine assesses:
- **Hazard Probability** — Likelihood of occurrence based on historical data and models.
- **Hazard Intensity** — Expected magnitude/intensity.
- **Exposure** — People, infrastructure, and systems in hazard zone.
- **Vulnerability** — Susceptibility of exposed elements to damage.
- **Risk** — Probability × Intensity × Exposure × Vulnerability.

## Data Sources

- Historical hazard databases (EM-DAT, NGDC, USGS).
- Geological and seismic monitoring networks.
- Meteorological and climate models.
- Satellite monitoring of active hazards.
- Early warning systems (tsunami, hurricane, flood).

## Hazard Maps

- Global hazard probability maps for each hazard type.
- Multi-hazard intersection maps.
- Hazard intensity return period maps.
- Exposure and vulnerability maps.
- Dynamic maps updated with new observations.
