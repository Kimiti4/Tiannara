# Civilizational Risk Model

## Purpose

Identify, classify, and track civilizational risks across all future world models, linking risks to evidence and uncertainty.

## Risk Categories

### Scientific Uncertainty
- Fundamental knowledge gaps
- Theory incompleteness
- Experimental limitations
- Observational constraints

### Engineering Failure
- Infrastructure reliability
- System complexity
- Safety margins
- Performance degradation

### Knowledge Bottlenecks
- Critical path dependencies
- Missing prerequisite knowledge
- Computational limitations
- Mathematical barriers

### Resource Constraints
- Scientific capital limits
- Energy availability
- Material availability
- Personnel capacity

### Governance Instability
- Institutional fragility
- Decision paralysis
- Constitutional violations
- Coordination failures

### Ecological Disruption
- Climate system changes
- Biodiversity loss
- Resource depletion
- Ecosystem service degradation

### Technological Misuse
- Dual-use research
- Safety failures
- Unintended consequences
- Control system failures

### Existential Threats
- Civilization-ending events
- Irreversible damage
- Permanent knowledge loss
- Human extinction risks

## Risk Properties

Each risk has:
- **Risk ID**: Content-addressed identifier
- **Category**: Risk classification
- **Evidence Basis**: Supporting evidence
- **Probability**: Estimated likelihood (0.0–1.0)
- **Impact**: Estimated severity (0.0–1.0)
- **Time Horizon**: When risk becomes relevant
- **Mitigation Options**: Known interventions
- **Confidence**: Confidence in risk assessment
- **Status**: Active/Mitigated/Realized/Archived

## Risk Tracking

- Risks tracked across all future worlds
- Risk probabilities updated with new evidence
- Mitigation effectiveness monitored
- Emerging risks detected and classified

## Constraints

- All risks must be evidence-linked
- Risk probabilities must have uncertainty bounds
- Risk records become constitutional artifacts
- Existential risks receive highest priority
