# Research Mission Architecture (Phase 21.0)

## Purpose

Define the architecture for research missions — bounded scientific efforts within research programs. Missions are the execution unit of the research civilization.

## Mission Lifecycle

```
Proposal
    ↓ (Scientific objectives, methodology, budget, schedule)
Approval
    ↓ (Council review, capital allocation)
Scheduling
    ↓ (Resource allocation, dependency resolution)
Execution
    ↓ (Hypothesis, experiment, analysis, theory)
Validation
    ↓ (Independent reproducibility verification)
Integration
    ↓ (Knowledge graph integration, capital assessment)
Archive
    ↓ (Complete replay and archaeology deposit)
```

## Mission Components

- **Scientific Objectives** — Specific, measurable objectives
- **Methodology** — Deterministic research methodology specification
- **Capital Budget** — Scientific capital allocated to the mission
- **Scheduled Duration** — Expected number of generations
- **Dependencies** — Required prior discoveries or missions
- **Collaborators** — Collaborating institutes, programs, or missions
- **Discoveries** — Discoveries produced (populated during execution)

## Mission Validation

Every mission must pass validation before integration:
1. Results independently reproduced
2. Evidence chains complete
3. Replay chain continuous
4. Archaeology deposit complete
5. Capital accounting reconciled

## Mission Archaeology

Each mission produces:
- Complete replay chain of all operations
- Complete archaeology of all artifacts
- Capital accounting record
- Validation certificate
- Integration record
