# Epistemic Ecosystem Model

## Purpose

Model the research civilization as a living epistemic ecosystem where knowledge, institutions, collaborations, and discoveries interact through constitutional dynamics.

## Ecosystem Components

### Knowledge Nodes
- Scientific theories
- Mathematical theorems
- Experimental results
- Engineering artifacts
- World models
- Each node has provenance, evidence, and certification status

### Institutional Organisms
- Research institutes as semi-autonomous agents
- Each with charter, mandate, portfolio, resources
- Capable of evolution, collaboration, competition
- Bounded by constitutional constraints

### Collaboration Symbioses
- Mutualistic: Both institutes benefit equally
- Commensalistic: One benefits, other unaffected
- Parasitic (prevented): One benefits at other's expense
- Competitive: Institutes pursuing overlapping goals

### Knowledge Flow
- Discoveries propagate through the ecosystem
- Knowledge exchange rate varies by relationship strength
- Some knowledge remains local (within institute)
- Foundational knowledge becomes global (civilization-wide)

### Ecosystem Niches
- Each domain represents an epistemic niche
- Institutes occupy primary and secondary niches
- Niche overlap indicates competition or collaboration
- Empty niches represent unexplored domains

## Ecosystem Health Indicators

### Biodiversity
- Number of distinct epistemic approaches
- Variety of active paradigms
- Diversity of methodologies

### Resilience
- Ability to absorb discovery shocks
- Recovery from incorrect theories
- Adaptation to paradigm shifts

### Productivity
- Discovery rate per institute
- Knowledge frontier advancement
- Cross-niche pollination rate

### Stability
- Institutional turnover rate
- Collaboration network robustness
- Knowledge retention across generations

## Ecosystem Evolution

The ecosystem evolves through:
- Niche creation (new domains)
- Niche partitioning (domain specialization)
- Niche convergence (domain integration)
- Symbiosis formation (collaboration)
- Competitive exclusion (rare, constitutionally bounded)

## Constraints

- Ecosystem model must be deterministic
- All ecosystem interactions must be replayable
- Ecosystem health must be measurable
- Ecosystem records become constitutional artifacts
- No ecosystem mechanism may override constitutional guarantees
