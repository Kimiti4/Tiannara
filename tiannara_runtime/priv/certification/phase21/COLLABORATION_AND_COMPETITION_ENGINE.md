# Collaboration and Competition Engine

## Purpose

Define the constitutional framework for managing the dual dynamics of collaboration and competition across research institutes, ensuring healthy scientific rivalry that advances civilization knowledge.

## Collaboration Mechanisms

### Joint Research Programs
- Multi-institute programs with shared objectives
- Defined contribution and attribution terms
- Joint milestone tracking and reporting

### Resource Pools
- Shared compute clusters and simulation environments
- Co-funded infrastructure development
- Shared engineering facilities

### Knowledge Commons
- Open mathematical and theoretical foundations
- Shared experimental datasets
- Common simulation platforms

### Personnel Exchange
- Cross-institute researcher visits
- Joint supervision of projects
- Expertise sharing programs

## Competition Mechanisms

### Scientific Priority
- First-to-discover recognition
- Concurrent independent verification
- Priority attribution in archaeology

### Resource Competition
- Performance-based resource allocation
- Discovery rate influence on budgets
- Efficiency metrics for capital allocation

### Reputation
- Peer review outcomes
- Discovery impact metrics
- Collaboration value contribution

### Alternative Approaches
- Competing theoretical frameworks
- Alternative experimental methodologies
- Competing engineering solutions

## Balance Principles

- Competition must never impede civilization progress
- Collaboration must be voluntary and mutually beneficial
- Competitive advantage must be evidence-based
- No institute may monopolize a research domain
- Competition results must be replayable and archaeological

## Conflict Resolution

### Dispute Escalation
1. Direct institute negotiation
2. Federation mediation
3. Constitutional arbitration
4. CSOS v1.0 review

### Resolution Principles
- Evidence-based resolution
- Preservation of institutional autonomy
- Minimum intervention principle
- All resolutions recorded in archaeology

## Constraints

- Competition must remain constitutional
- Collaboration must preserve institute identity
- Conflicts must be resolved deterministically
- All competitive interactions are recorded and replayable
- No competition may compromise civilization-wide discovery sharing
