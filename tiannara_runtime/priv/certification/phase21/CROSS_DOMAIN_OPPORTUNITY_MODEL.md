# Cross-Domain Opportunity Model

## Purpose

Define mechanisms for discovering scientific opportunities that emerge from the interaction of multiple domains, enabling interdisciplinary research that would not be visible from within a single domain.

## Cross-Domain Opportunity Types

### Domain Boundary Phenomena
- Phenomena that exist at the boundary between two domains
- Examples: Quantum biology, socio-physics, computational linguistics
- Detection: Frontier layer discontinuities between adjacent domains

### Convergent Questions
- Same question asked independently in multiple domains
- Different domain-specific formulations of same underlying problem
- Detection: Knowledge graph structural similarity across domains

### Methodology Gaps
- Methodology effective in one domain but absent in another
- Technique transfer opportunity
- Detection: Cross-domain methodology presence comparison

### Theoretical Tensions
- Incompatible theoretical frameworks between domains
- Domain A predicts, Domain B contradicts
- Detection: Cross-domain contradiction registry

### Emergent Phenomena
- Phenomena that only appear through domain interaction
- Complex systems at domain intersections
- Detection: Simulation divergence at domain boundaries

### Unifying Opportunities
- Potential for unified theory across domains
- Mathematical structure common to multiple domains
- Detection: Cross-domain mathematical pattern matching

## Cross-Domain Detection Process

### Step 1: Domain Pair Analysis
- Analyze each pair of active domains
- Compute frontier proximity and tension metrics
- Identify boundary interaction density

### Step 2: Cross-Domain Tension Measurement
- Measure theoretical incompatibility score
- Compute methodology transfer potential
- Estimate unification benefit

### Step 3: Opportunity Formation
- Form cross-domain opportunity at identified intersection
- Document contributing domains and their roles
- Assign lead and supporting domain designations
- Record cross-domain provenance

## Provenance Preservation

Every cross-domain opportunity records:
- All contributing domains
- Detection mechanism for each domain contribution
- Cross-domain evidence chain
- Domain-specific knowledge dependencies

## Constraints

- Cross-domain opportunities must involve at least 2 domains
- Detection mechanisms must be deterministic
- Provenance must be complete and replayable
- Cross-domain opportunities must be prioritized within the same framework as single-domain opportunities
