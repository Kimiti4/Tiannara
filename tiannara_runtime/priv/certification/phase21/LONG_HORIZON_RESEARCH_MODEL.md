# Long-Horizon Research Model

## Purpose

Define the constitutional model for long-horizon research planning, enabling the civilization to formulate scientific strategies spanning decades while preserving determinism and replayability.

## Horizon Tiers

| Tier | Span | Detail Level | Update Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Near-term | 1–5 years | High resolution | Per generation |
| Medium-term | 5–25 years | Moderate resolution | Per 5 generations |
| Long-term | 25–100 years | Low resolution | Per 10 generations |
| Multi-generational | 100–500 years | Directional | Per 25 generations |
| Century-scale | 500–1000 years | Visionary | Per 100 generations |

## Strategic Horizon Elements

Each horizon tier contains:
- **Strategic Goals**: Civilizational-scale objectives
- **Milestones**: Evidence of progress toward goals
- **Uncertainty Bounds**: Explicit confidence intervals per horizon
- **Knowledge Dependencies**: Required discoveries mapped
- **Resource Requirements**: Estimated scientific capital needs
- **Risk Assessments**: Identified strategic risks

## Horizon Interaction

- Near-term goals derive from medium-term strategy
- Medium-term strategy derives from long-term vision
- Long-term vision remains stable across generations
- Short-term discoveries may revise long-term expectations
- All horizon transitions are recorded and replayable

## Determinism

Horizon projections must be deterministic given current knowledge state. Different horizons may have different uncertainty levels, but the projection methodology must produce identical outputs for identical inputs.

## Constraints

- Every strategic element must have an explicit time horizon
- Uncertainty must increase with horizon distance
- Horizon updates must be evidence-driven
- Horizon transitions must be replayable
- Strategic horizon records become constitutional artifacts
