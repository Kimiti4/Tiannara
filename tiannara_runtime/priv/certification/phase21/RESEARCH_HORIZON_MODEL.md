# Research Horizon Model

## Purpose

Define the constitutional framework for managing research across multiple time horizons, ensuring appropriate strategic depth at each timescale while maintaining coherence across all horizons.

## Horizon Architecture

```
Civilizational Vision (500+ years)
    ↓
Century-scale Strategy (100–500 years)
    ↓
Multi-generational Plans (25–100 years)
    ↓
Long-term Roadmaps (10–25 years)
    ↓
Medium-term Programs (5–10 years)
    ↓
Near-term Missions (1–5 years)
    ↓
Current Execution (present)
```

## Horizon Properties

### Resolution
- Near-term: High granularity (individual missions)
- Medium-term: Program-level granularity
- Long-term: Roadmap-level granularity
- Century-scale: Directional objectives only

### Uncertainty
- Near-term: Low uncertainty (well-characterized)
- Medium-term: Moderate uncertainty (bounded)
- Long-term: High uncertainty (directional)
- Century-scale: Very high uncertainty (visionary)

### Update Frequency
- Near-term: Per generation
- Medium-term: Per 5 generations
- Long-term: Per 10–25 generations
- Century-scale: Per 100 generations or paradigm shift

## Horizon Coherence

- Lower horizons must derive from higher horizons
- Higher horizons constrain but do not dictate lower horizons
- Horizon violations (lower horizon contradicting higher) trigger constitutional review
- Discovery at any horizon may trigger revision at all dependent horizons

## Constraints

- Every strategic element must belong to exactly one horizon
- Horizon boundaries must be clearly defined
- Coherence across horizons must be verifiable
- Horizon transitions must be replayable
- Horizon records become constitutional artifacts
