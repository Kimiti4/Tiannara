# Mission Success Model

## Purpose

Define how mission success is measured, recorded, and evaluated within the constitutional research framework.

## Success Dimensions

### Knowledge Gain (0.0–1.0)
- Actual vs. expected knowledge gained
- Novel results produced
- Open questions resolved
- Predictive accuracy improved

### Scientific Impact (0.0–1.0)
- Citations and derivative work
- Paradigm influence
- Methodology adoption
- Cross-domain reach

### Engineering Applicability (0.0–1.0)
- Engineered artifacts produced
- Infrastructure improvements
- Instrumentation advances
- Technology readiness progressed

### Mathematical Contribution (0.0–1.0)
- New theorems established
- Formal proofs completed
- Mathematical frameworks extended
- Computational methods developed

### Cross-Domain Influence (0.0–1.0)
- Domains affected
- Interdisciplinary discoveries
- Bridge concepts established
- Cross-domain methodology exported

### Civilizational Importance (0.0–1.0)
- Societal benefit
- Long-term value
- Civilizational capability enhanced
- Knowledge preserved for future generations

## Composite Success Score

```
Success = (KG × 0.25) + (SI × 0.20) + (EA × 0.15) + (MC × 0.15) + (CD × 0.15) + (CI × 0.10)
```

## Success Classification

| Score Range | Classification |
|---|---|
| 0.85–1.00 | Outstanding |
| 0.70–0.84 | Successful |
| 0.50–0.69 | Partially Successful |
| 0.30–0.49 | Limited Success |
| 0.00–0.29 | Unsuccessful |

## Replay Convergence

Mission success replay must reconstruct identical success scores when replaying from the same initial conditions. Deviation indicates archaeological inconsistency.

## Constraints

- Success must be measured against predefined criteria
- No post-hoc adjustment of success thresholds
- All success data becomes constitutional artifacts
- Success records must be replayable and auditable
